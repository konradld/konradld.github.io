**Abstract:** Regression estimates can be precisely estimated, yet rest on a small share of the data. We study the population tipping fraction, the smallest share whose exclusion overturns a stated conclusion with reference adjustments held fixed. Ranking observations by their support for the conclusion yields the minimum sample deletion count, and interpolation estimates the population share. We derive its limiting distribution and construct a confidence interval that accounts for using the same sample to fit the adjustments, estimate a relative threshold, and identify the most supportive observations. In the Mindspark experiment, excluding 31 of 535 students cuts the estimated math gain by at least half. The nominal 95% interval for the tipping fraction is 3.36--8.60%, and falls below a 13.6% attrition benchmark.
